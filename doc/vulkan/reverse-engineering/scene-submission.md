# Scene submission contract recovered from source

Revision inspected: `59108e15a1f8f94d2da7c674d937d19f5cf9450d` (verified HEAD). Repository was clean on entry. Read-only inspection; no viewer execution or GPU capture. Paths below are repository-relative, with **1-based source lines**. Pipeline scheduling, global render-state setup, backend implementation, shader math, and probe orchestration belong to the other reports; references to their interfaces here are observations of scene-side callers, not independent analysis of their implementations.

## 1. Architectural boundary

- The submission graph is **viewer object → drawable → faces → spatial group → typed draw-info vectors → pool consumer**. `LLSpatialGroup` owns pass-keyed vectors of reference-counted `LLDrawInfo` plus buffer maps; a draw info contains vertex/index range, material/texture references, transforms, avatar/skin identity and blend/coverage data. It is not simply a mesh + texture. Sources: `indra\newview\llspatialpartition.h:75-144,221-227`; `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:5600-5640,5771-5887`.
- There are two submission models: `LLRenderPass` consumes render-map batches, while special `LLFacePool`s retain references and receive a transient `mDrawFace` list (`enqueue`, cleared by `resetDrawOrders`). Terrain, trees, water, built-in avatars and sky use the latter rather than the general volume-batch path. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpool.cpp:62-125,270-295,434-449`; `indra\newview\lldrawpool.h:402-418`.
- Pool enum order is explicitly intended to prioritize opaque/large occluders before masked/blended surfaces. Actual enum sequence: old sky, water exclusion, WL sky, simple, fullbright, bump, materials, PBR opaque, terrain, grass, PBR mask, tree, alpha mask, fullbright alpha mask, avatar/control avatar, glow, pre-water alpha, void water, water, post-water alpha. `POOL_ALPHA` is a **classification only**, with no real pool; the two water-relative consumers share its faces. Do not treat enum order as proof of the complete frame sequence: phase pass counts and pipeline scheduling also matter. Source: `indra\newview\lldrawpool.h:49-79`.
- Every paired rigged pass must equal its non-rigged pass + 1. `registerFace()` enforces this numerically, rather than with a separate rigging queue abstraction. Source: `indra\newview\lldrawpool.h:134-204`; `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:5629-5640`.

**Native-renderer implication:** preserve the distinction between persistent scene/geometry state and per-view visibility/submission products. A replacement may change container layout and batching limits, but must retain material family, coverage mode, auxiliary glow/bump contributions, coordinate space, skin identity, and view-dependent ordering.

## 2. Visibility, hierarchy, bounds and occlusion

### Partitions and bridges

- Static volumes and non-volume objects use their pipeline-selected region partition. Active root linksets acquire a local spatial bridge: HUD first, then control-avatar/animesh, avatar attachment, ordinary moving volume; children use the parent's partition. Transitions destroy obsolete bridges. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawable.cpp:1204-1285`; `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:5425-5477`.
- Making a drawable active activates parent and children, queues volume rebuild and repartitions. Making it static is prohibited for `ACTIVE_CHILD`, attachments, flexible volumes and animated objects; an eligible transition clears active/animated-child state, recursively makes children static, destroys the bridge and queues rebuild. Source: `indra\newview\lldrawable.cpp:505-613`.
- Drawable transform matrices intentionally contain **no scale**; scale is baked in geometry-relative transforms. Active root geometry is root-relative, child geometry can be parent-relative, static geometry is region-relative; animated children can require independent world matrices. Rigged submissions ignore object model matrices. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawable.cpp:692-744`; `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:2132-2234,5673-5689`.
- Octree residence is not an exact visual bounding radius: volume binning considers near alpha/shrink-wrap state, otherwise at least `OctreeStaticObjectSizeFactor`, and clamps radius to `[0.5,256]`. Visual bounds are generated from face bounds. Rigged geometry with an avatar is deliberately positioned at group-space zero to co-locate it for batching. Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:2001-2115,4812-4869`.
- Moving within a node can update bounds in place; otherwise partition movement removes/reinserts the entry. Insertion marks object/geometry dirty, invalidates occlusion queries for all cameras, and schedules rebuild. Changing a volume's group clears all face buffer references: every non-null face buffer must belong to its current group. Sources: `indra\newview\llspatialpartition.cpp:230-249,305-325,948-1034`; `indra\newview\lldrawable.cpp:1168-1197`.
- Bounds dirtiness propagates upward. A one-child empty node copies the child bounds and marks that child as eligible to skip redundant frustum checks. Animesh bridge-root bounds are additionally expanded by the control avatar's animated extents. Sources: `indra\newview\llvieweroctree.cpp:516-570`; `indra\newview\llspatialpartition.cpp:841-870`.

### Culling semantics

- Normal group tests combine AABB/frustum **without far plane** with a sphere intersection at camera frustum-corner distance. Shadow culling uses the full frustum; infinite-far partitions or disabled far clip use only no-far-plane AABB checks, except cube snapshots retain their clip policy. Selection traversal bypasses occlusion. Sources: `indra\newview\llspatialpartition.cpp:1081-1148,1260-1293,1428-1480`.
- Normal cull traversal first checks previous occlusion results; an occluded non-root node is registered as an occluder and its subtree skipped. Reflection rendering bypasses this early occlusion rejection. A visible group is handed to `gPipeline.markNotCulled`; the nearby inline `doOcclusion()` block is commented out, not an active query-issue path. Source: `indra\newview\llspatialpartition.cpp:1054-1113`.
- Once a bridge's outer bounds pass camera tests, the implementation traverses its entire internal octree with `LLOctreeMarkNotCulled`, not another per-child frustum/occlusion culler. Normal non-shadow/non-impostor views reject bridges below 16 projected pixels. Non-HUD attachment bridges additionally require a visible, fully loaded, recently seen non-impostor parent avatar. Cameras are transformed into bridge-local coordinates for internal submission and distance work. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawable.cpp:91,1416-1442,1493-1608`.
- Visibility has frame history: drawable visibility may be inherited from the group's current stamp; “recently visible” spans current/previous frame. Groups keep camera-indexed visibility and occlusion state. Do not replace these semantics with a single permanent visible bit. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawable.cpp:1133-1165`; `indra\newview\llvieweroctree.cpp:405-441,874-904`.

### Occlusion contract

- Occlusion state is per camera. `sUseOcclusion < 2` does not read/modify query results here; `1` is effectively read-only use of already-known state, `2+` permits updates. Parent occlusion implicitly covers descendants. A discarded query is released and visibility restored down the affected subtree. Available positive results clear occlusion; zero sets it. The `RenderOcclusionTimeout` fallback performs a potentially blocking result read after too many availability checks. Source: `indra\newview\llvieweroctree.cpp:1107-1170`.
- Queries test a conservatively expanded group box, not the full mesh: ordinary half extents +0.25; void-water Z +1. The camera-inside test uses +0.5 and forces visible/discards queries. Water/void-water query geometry uses depth clamp. Queries are not reissued while pending unless discarded; issue frame and camera are recorded. Sources: `indra\newview\llvieweroctree.cpp:140,1058-1099,1174-1293`.
- `LLSpatialPartition::cull(camera,bool do_occlusion)` does **not use its boolean argument** in the inspected body; global state and visitor behavior govern occlusion. This API parameter is not a reliable replacement-renderer toggle. Source: `indra\newview\llspatialpartition.cpp:1451-1480`.

**Native-renderer implication:** preserve conservative visibility, camera-specific history, bridge/attachment dependencies and invalidation when geometry moves. The exact query API, synchronous timeout and octree storage are implementation choices, not visual requirements; stale negative visibility must not survive relevant mutations.

## 3. LOD and update cadence

- Geometry and distance/LOD are intentionally not refreshed indiscriminately for every view. Group and drawable `updateDistance()` reject non-world camera IDs and skip origin-shift frames. Attached objects use animated avatar-box distances in drawable distance updates; impostor attachments skip bridge distance updates outside the impostor render. Sources: `indra\newview\llspatialpartition.cpp:615-641`; `indra\newview\lldrawable.cpp:873-962,1612-1653`.
- A volume group/bridge has LOD period 32 and slop ratio 0.25. Groups get a staggered modulo-frame seed. `changeLOD()` fires for object/alpha dirty state, sufficient relative distance change, or the group's periodic slot. Update urgency is zero when invisible, otherwise age + size²/distance. Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:5443-5464`; `indra\newview\llvieweroctree.cpp:864-891`; `indra\newview\llspatialpartition.cpp:728-778`.
- Dynamic volume LOD quantizes `lod_factor*radius/distance` to 0.01 and selects among four levels using thresholds 0.03, 0.06, 0.24 (the last table entry is a sentinel-like unused upper bound in this selector). Non-dynamic selection clamps `sqrt(radius)*lod_factor*4` to 0..3. Distance is rescaled, quadratically reduced (`distance²/rampDist`) in the near ramp, and multiplied by π/3; FOV optionally scales the LOD factor. HUDs force LOD 3, selection may use a debug override. Native GLTF assets skip this volume LOD path. Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:1608-1621,1664-1790`; `indra\llmath\llvolumemgr.cpp:33-42,328-339`.
- Rigged volume LOD uses avatar distance and animated extents: half-diagonal for control avatars, **full diagonal** for regular avatar attachments to preserve historic behavior. Other volumes use LOD scale bias × object scale. An actual LOD change queues volume rebuild; a >10% bin-radius change without LOD change queues partition movement. Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:1683-1728,1836-1873`.
- Rebuilds distinguish geometry/classification changes from vertex-only changes. `rebuildGeom()` runs only for geometry/alpha dirty, otherwise delegates mesh dirty; it clears/rebuilds draw maps and face references. `rebuildMesh()` updates existing face ranges and flushes buffers, falling back to a full geometry rebuild if accounting fails. Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:5927-5980,6168-6173,6480-6572`.
- LOD decreases do not automatically shrink octree bounds: new higher LOD or sculpt changes request bounds updates, explicitly avoiding a LOD/bounds feedback loop. Position/rigged changes can independently force bounds updates. Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:2261-2296,2304-2415`.
- Face pixel-area computation is throttled to roughly 0.1 seconds with up to 10% jitter. Rigged texture footprints aggregate joint-transformed bounds, not necessarily CPU-skinned vertices. Texture virtual area accounts for UV extents, scale, sculpt exception, importance and partial overlap. Nonvisible ordinary volume texture updates set face area/virtual size to zero; cube snapshots skip updates; HUD textures are treated as full-screen. Sources: `indra\newview\llface.cpp:2351-2521`; `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:878-999`.
- Texture animation uses a live per-face matrix only above virtual size 16; creating/crossing that threshold forces texcoord and group draw-info rebuilds because the matrix changes batch eligibility. Initial animation also shrink-wraps the object to localize rebuild costs. Sources: `indra\newview\llface.h:57-60`; `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:723-808,976-1000`.

The caller's absolute per-frame rebuild budgets, avatar animation cadence and texture scheduler cadence are outside this report. “Period 32” is an eligibility cadence, **not proof that every visible object's geometry is rebuilt every 32 frames**.

## 4. Geometry generation, material routing and batching

### Geometry payload and eligibility

- Geometry rebuild skips dead/forced-invisible drawables, native `mGLTFAsset` objects (separate scene manager), unavailable mesh assets/disabled mesh rez and skin information still loading. It refreshes material textures and applies queued GLTF overrides before classification. Optional Firestorm surface-area protection can suppress high-cost volumes. Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:6011-6088`.
- Rigging means either an attached mesh with skin info, or an animated rigged mesh with a playing control avatar. Skin/avatar pointers are installed on faces and rigged/unrigged buckets are separate. First rigged classification triggers another LOD update. Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:6092-6128,6175-6200,6393-6412`.
- Buckets distinguish simple, fullbright, alpha, legacy bump, normal, specular, normal+specular, and PBR layouts. Each has separate rigged/unrigged lists, capped at 4096 faces per list. Vertex buffers obey `RenderMaxVBOSize` and a 65535-vertex ceiling; node geometry has a separate `RenderMaxNodeSize` budget. Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:5526-5535,5905-5924,5991-5995,6203-6207,6417-6469,6638-6641`.
- General lit layout requires position/normal/color/UV0; normal maps add tangent+UV1, specular adds UV2; PBR requires position/normal/color/tangent/UV0. Indexed texture selection and rigged weight4 are added as needed. Any TE glow causes an emissive byte stream to be included across the group's layouts. Source: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:6417-6474`.
- Geometry's semantic payload includes pass-sensitive color alpha: ordinary non-alpha legacy faces without a specular map repurpose it for shiny levels 0/.25/.5/.75; alpha faces must retain transparency. PBR vertex color derives from PBR base color instead of legacy TE color. TE glow occupies emissive alpha. Sources: `indra\newview\llface.cpp:1459-1497,2259-2277`.
- UV transforms for legacy diffuse/normal/specular can be baked separately; PBR ordinary geometry leaves material transforms for later. Rigged vertices use bind-shape transformation, normals inverse-transpose of bind shape, and preserve tangent handedness/weight4 data. Sources: `indra\newview\llface.cpp:1536-1652,2193-2229`.

### Effective pass classification

Initial pool selection delegates to `gPipeline.getPoolTypeFromTE()`, which the parent report owns. The **subsequent definitive pass emission** is here:

| Surface condition | Emitted semantic pass |
|---|---|
| GLTF material present | Overrides traditional material/shiny classification: BLEND → `PASS_ALPHA`, MASK → `PASS_GLTF_PBR_ALPHA_MASK`, otherwise `PASS_GLTF_PBR`. |
| Legacy material + TE fullbright | Explicit mask and opaque TE alpha → fullbright mask; transparent TE or blend → alpha; otherwise fullbright unless environment intensity/shiny requires material evaluation. |
| Legacy material, non-fullbright | TE alpha `<0.999` → alpha; emboss bump without normal map can go bump; otherwise material shader-mask table. |
| Material shader-mask table | Four map families (none/specular/normal/both) × opaque/mask/emissive-alpha. Every BLEND table slot maps to shared `PASS_ALPHA`, not old family-specific blend pass enums. |
| No material, alpha-classified | Fully transparent highlight geometry → `PASS_ALPHA_INVISIBLE`; eligible automatic mask → ordinary/fullbright mask; otherwise shared alpha. |
| Legacy opaque shiny | Static world fullbright → fullbright-shiny, potentially extra post-bump; lit emboss → bump; other lit shiny → simple (simple deferred includes shiny). |
| Legacy opaque without shiny | Fullbright → fullbright, potentially extra post-bump; lit emboss → bump; otherwise simple. |
| Old alpha-only/invisiprim texture | Only non-attachment, non-rigged geometry emits `PASS_INVISIBLE` (shiny case may also emit old invisi-shiny). This now represents water exclusion. |
| Non-alpha + TE glow | Additional `PASS_GLOW` or `PASS_GLTF_GLOW`, not replacement for the base pass. |

Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:6916-7257`. Important defensive quirks: a material claiming blend in a nondistance-sorted list is forced to NONE, and a specular shader-mask bit is dropped if the built layout lacks UV2 (`7060-7082`). These are compatibility fallbacks, not a principled material-model rule.

- Automatic alpha-to-mask conversion is forbidden for PBR, rigged faces, explicit legacy BLEND, HUDs, non-unit TE alpha, or TE glow. It additionally requires the texture's alpha-mask classification and the appropriate deferred/fullbright automask setting. `sNoAlpha` overrides most checks after PBR rejection. Source: `indra\newview\llface.cpp:1211-1257`.
- Completely transparent faces normally never enter the alpha bucket unless glow/debug/beacon behavior requires them. A documented PBR rigged routing inconsistency has an additional zero-base-alpha rejection in the otherwise opaque bucket path. Source: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:6270-6339`.
- The 512-pixel “force simple” condition affects initial pool selection, but it is **not a blanket guarantee of removing material evaluation**; the later material/PBR branches still inspect the TE. Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:98,6228-6239,6315-6377,6916-6980`.

### Batch boundaries and ordering

- Non-alpha faces sort by bump, fullbright, legacy material ID, shiny, texture pointer, then stable original draw-order index; rigged opaque faces prepend avatar and skin hash. Unrigged alpha faces sort descending projected face depth. **Rigged alpha preserves original face order**, because the rigged+distance-sort branch deliberately performs no sort. Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:6576-6663`; face projected-depth calculation: `indra\newview\lldrawable.cpp:907-925`.
- Indexed texture batches can contain multiple diffuse textures up to shader channel capacity (local array maximum 32), but bump maps, nonempty materials, legacy alpha override cases, alpha-only textures, active texture animation, and PBR break texture batching. Alpha batching reuses existing texture slots without reordering faces. Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:5480-5523,6708-6799`.
- Actual draw-call coalescing only extends the **last draw info in that pass** when buffer/range are contiguous and texture compatibility, material hash, fullbright, bump, material-specific shiny, texture-matrix pointer, model-matrix pointer, shader mask, avatar and skin hash all match. PBR material hash replaces legacy material hash; non-media PBR faces do not retain their unused diffuse texture as a batch key. Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:5701-5789`.
- New draw info captures specular/environment factors, maps, legacy alpha mode and cutoff. Default mask cutoff is .33, grass .5, legacy material cutoff is byte/255. PBR keeps the actual fetched material. Sources: `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:5803-5885`.

**Native-renderer implication:** material classification is multi-contribution, not one mesh→one pass. Reordering opaque batches is broadly flexible; rigged-alpha authored order, unrigged alpha depth order, water-side partition and glow interleaving are not. New batching should key *semantic* transforms/materials/skin palettes rather than copying raw pointer identities.

### SL PBR CPU material binding (distinct from native GLTF scenes)

- `LLFetchedGLTFMaterial::bind()` serves these volume PBR batches. Media overrides **both base-color and emissive textures**. Missing base color/ORM/emissive binds white; absent or insufficiently resident normal maps (discard level >4) bind flat normal. ORM is one linear packed texture, and the primitive material model aliases occlusion to metallic-roughness: independent occlusion images are not supported by this model. Sources: `indra\newview\llfetchedgltfmaterial.cpp:64-125`; `indra\llprimitive\llgltfmaterial.h:96-109`.
- Base-color factor is baked into the volume vertex stream, not uploaded here as an additional uniform; roughness, metallic and emissive factors are uniforms. Base-color, normal, ORM and emissive each get their own packed UV transform. Shadow rendering binds base-color and its transform only, omitting the other channels/factors. Sources: `indra\newview\llfetchedgltfmaterial.cpp:94-144`; `indra\newview\llface.cpp:1459-1497`.
- This binder writes `MINIMUM_ALPHA = alphaCutoff` for MASK, otherwise **-1** during non-shadow rendering, including BLEND; non-mask shadows leave the caller's threshold untouched. Thus alpha-pool shader initialization (.004 or impostor .1) is not necessarily the final per-draw PBR threshold. Do not merge legacy-alpha and SL-PBR cutoff policies, or infer the separate native-GLTF shader's cutoff arithmetic from this helper. Source: `indra\newview\llfetchedgltfmaterial.cpp:64-83`.
- Default SL material values are base color `(1,1,1,1)`, metallic=roughness=1, cutoff=.5, emissive=0, opaque mode, single-sided, identity UV transforms. The constructor zeroes members before setting nonzero defaults. Draw helpers disable face culling for double-sided PBR, including untextured draws. Sources: `indra\llprimitive\llgltfmaterial.cpp:50-81`; `indra\newview\lldrawpool.cpp:822-853`.
- Simple legacy alpha binds flat-normal/white-specular fallbacks; explicit legacy material alpha binds its available normal/spec maps instead. Its transform can still use the texture matrix. This differs from the explicit channel-by-channel fallback policy of the PBR binder. Source: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:708-760`.

## 5. Alpha, water separation and the branch's newer transparency paths

### Shared alpha contract

- Both alpha pools expose one post-deferred pass. Pre-water selects the opposite side of the water plane from post-water; underwater flips the sign. Entire groups are rejected only when wholly on the wrong side, using bridge extents when appropriate; shaders receive the water plane/sign for fragment-level splitting. HUDs disable this clipping. Opaque-water clipping can skip pre-water alpha entirely. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:91-94,116-126,146-165,896-985`.
- Alpha shader initialization sets minimum alpha .004; impostors .1, subject to per-material overrides described above. Custom blend equations with neither factor SOURCE_ALPHA temporarily permit zero-alpha fragments. `LLDrawInfo` defaults to source-over but carries per-draw RGB blend factors. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:67-71,128-135,1224-1245`; `indra\newview\llspatialpartition.h:138-139`.
- Legacy order is rigged alpha first, then world alpha. Rigged alpha writes depth, world alpha ordinarily only tests it; pre-water alpha, impostor alpha-depth passes and `sSkipScreenCopy` also force writes. A native GLTF-scene hook renders depth before post-water rigged alpha. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:379-387,428-467`.
- Alpha's RGB is source-over (unless batch override); destination alpha is **glow**, not conventional accumulated surface opacity: source factor ZERO, destination factor ONE_MINUS_SOURCE_ALPHA suppresses glow behind transparency. Glow is then added via a separate RGB-preserving, alpha-additive replay of emissive geometry; queues are flushed per group and separated into PBR/legacy and rigged/static. Pre-water does not enqueue these glow draws. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:444-448,1248-1335`.
- PBR alpha selects PBR shading, legacy fullbright uses fullbright shading, lit legacy can select material-shader variants; HUDs force their own path. PBR double-sided disables culling; particles disable culling and honor a separate visibility toggle. Matrix palettes must be refreshed on shader transitions. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:1013-1048,1093-1222`; `indra\newview\lldrawpool.cpp:722-755`.

### Optional interleaved alpha (not universal)

- Only the eligible post-water consumer switches the shared group lists into interleaved sort order; pre-water retains legacy order. World attachment groups use stamped avatar depth rather than their own group depth. Rigged groups use avatar depth then avatar identity then descending render-order then bounds depth. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:170-173,367-376`; `indra\newview\llspatialpartition.h:253-341`.
- The merged walk picks the farther stream head and drains avatar ensembles contiguously. Ties choose rigged before that avatar's unrigged attachments; plain world groups composite after same-depth ensembles. Depth writes in merged mode are enabled for stamped rigged groups, not unstamped rigged groups, unless a global write condition applies. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:877-999`.
- Therefore interleaving is **avatar-ensemble ordering**, not per-triangle transparency sorting, and not simply a single distance-sorted list of all draw infos.

### PPLL / bounded depth peeling present at this HEAD

- `RenderAlphaSortMethod` 1 selects PPLL, 2 selects depth peeling, contingent on resources/support. Eligibility is specifically post-water, main render target, non-HUD, non-impostor, non-reflection, non-cube-snapshot. Other views stay on stock paths. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:211-244`.
- PPLL captures rigged then world source-over alpha, composites, and then replays rigged alpha color-disabled to restore the legacy scene-depth contribution before residual alpha/glow. Capture must not write shared scene depth or fragments behind transparent surfaces would be lost before resolve. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:264-295,420-436`.
- Depth peel executes select + replay for rigged/world at every exact layer, then one legacy-order tail for nearer fragments beyond the exact-layer budget. Layer setting clamps 1..32, default 8; CPU submission time budget clamps 1..50 ms, default 4 ms. Teleport or >100 ms frame suppresses peeling until two seconds without transient overload. Failures fall back to ordinary rigged/world rendering. Source: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:246-262,306-365`.
- **Rigged scene-depth restoration is PPLL-only, not a shared OIT operation.** Successful depth peeling goes directly from depth-write-disabled select/replay/tail to residual draws, with no counterpart to the explicit PPLL color-disabled rigged replay. Residual rendering can write depth for residual rigged/custom-blend geometry, but skips standard peeled source-over RGB draws and replays their glow without rigged depth writes; this does not restore the full peeled rigged depth surface. Stock fallback does regain stock depth behavior. Both OIT branches call `drawGLTFScene()` beforehand, but that is neither post-peel restoration nor presently effective mesh submission given §6a's disabled native batch production. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:264-365,428-441,489-500,815-839,1051-1089`.
- Particles and non-source-over custom blends are excluded from capture/peel and rendered in a residual stock phase. **Both lit and fullbright source-over alpha participate**. Captured surfaces skip duplicate residual RGB but still contribute additive glow there. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:1013-1090,1248-1296`.
- DoF caveat: `renderPostDeferred` labels a final color-disabled world-alpha traversal as depth-only, sets .33 on a fullbright-mask shader, and invokes `renderAlpha(...,true)`. **Color suppression is explicit and supported by the inspected call chain:** this invokes `renderAlpha` directly, not `forwardRender`, so it does not re-enable color writes, and emissive replay is suppressed. The final restore is RGB-on/alpha-off, matching the parent's reported incoming mask. The remaining uncertainty is depth-write inheritance and material-specific cutoff: `forwardRender`'s scoped depth state has already ended, and `renderAlpha` still selects PBR/legacy material shaders (`depth_only` principally suppresses glow). Do not elevate the nearby “90% transparent” comment or .33 into a universal effective threshold. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:391-408,413-487,1093-1239,1287-1290`; PBR cutoff override: `indra\newview\llfetchedgltfmaterial.cpp:64-83`.

### Terrain detail

- Ordinary terrain blends four diffuse detail textures with a clamped 2D alpha ramp. PBR/local override terrain uses four material slots and paint-type-specific shader selection. Optional quality levels enable normal, metallic-roughness and emissive maps; missing images use white or flat-normal defaults. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolterrain.cpp:218-240,244-317,350-455`.
- PBR terrain transforms use southwest region origin, U east, V south, global uniform detail scale, and **one transform per material**, taken from base-color transform, rather than independent per-channel transforms. Height/noise uses alpha ramp; paintmap is RGB differences from slot 1, black fallback selects slot 1. Material mask cutoffs are divided by base alpha before upload. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolterrain.cpp:461-575`.
- Terrain shadow is geometry-only here. Parcel ownership is a separate negative-offset highlight draw. The modern deferred entry calls `renderFullShader`; retained `renderFull4TU`, `renderFull2TU`, `renderSimple` are not selected by that entry. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolterrain.cpp:145-165,169-213,642-659`; no claim of global unreachability beyond this inspected call chain.

### Avatar detail

- The built-in avatar pool derives the avatar from its first queued face (or an explicit preview avatar), then calls `renderRigid()` for eyes and `renderSkinned()` for body. Deferred sets “skip transparent”; post-deferred sets “skip opaque”; minimum alpha defaults .2, impostor texture rendering uses .01. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolavatar.cpp:71,172-250,551-588,674-717,901-938`.
- Loaded state, friend-only policy, appearance/impostor state and parent-avatar visibility can suppress rendering; shadows reject dead/UI/friend-filtered, too-slow, impostored or invisible avatars. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolavatar.cpp:358-395,801-864`.
- Rigged attachment palettes come from avatar `updateSkinInfoMatrixPalette(skinInfo)`; an empty palette suppresses the draw. Normal batch loops cache avatar+skin hash, while mixed alpha additionally keys by shader. CPU `LLRiggedVolume` skinning is an auxiliary selected/picking/bounds path, not the general GPU attachment submission path. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpool.cpp:658-755`; `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:5180-5237,5240-5416`.
- Forward `beginSkinned()` explicitly says “preview only” and “deprecated logic”; ordinary world rendering uses deferred/post-deferred. Optional classic cloth provides avatar wind, ripple sine and gravity uniforms before skin draw. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolavatar.cpp:602-623,645-655,909-938`.

### Water and sky detail

- Water samples the copied pre-water scene/depth and exclusion texture, selects above/below-water shader, updates normals/environment uniforms every render and marks reflection/distortion update flags after drawing non-edge water. The two `pushWaterPlanes(pass)` calls in exclusion currently iterate the **same complete face list**, because the modern implementation ignores `pass`; do not infer a separate edge draw list from that legacy call pattern. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolwater.cpp:111-139,202-255,306-340`; `indra\newview\lldrawpoolwaterexclusion.cpp:56-65`.
- WL sky updates sky geometry at render time. The moon writes depth specifically to occlude stars; stars use additive-with-alpha and no depth writes; sky depth is cleared after the pool so later haze can identify unwritten scene depth. Cube snapshots omit stars and omit clouds for irradiance, retaining clouds for radiance. HDRI replaces atmospheric objects according to HDRI availability/split/capture policy. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolwlsky.cpp:84-93,132-181,219-237,290-301,354-358,471-498`.

## 6a. Native GLTF scene follow-up: compiled consumers, disabled draw-data production

**Decisive baseline limitation:** this native asset path is not equivalent to the active SL PBR material path. `Asset::prep()` computes per-material/per-variant offsets and resizes batch arrays, but **vertex-buffer allocation/upload is inside `#if 0`**, and **the entire node→primitive render-batch population loop is also inside `#if 0`**. The renderer rejects empty primitive lists or null buffers. Targeted searches found no alternative producer of these native `mRenderData` primitive lists/buffers. Therefore ordinarily loaded native assets at this HEAD do not reach the manager's mesh draw calls, despite active loader/update/picking and pool hooks. The following describes the compiled CPU data/consumer contract and latent limitations, not verified visible native-scene rendering. Sources: `indra\newview\gltf\asset.cpp:558-688`; `indra\newview\gltfscenemanager.cpp:655-676`.

- **Loading/update:** local loading attaches an asset to a selected root object and registers it with the manager; network GLTF sculpt assets register through `LLVOVolume`. Manager update visits all registered living assets, independent of volume octree culling. `Asset::update()` executes only for positive frame-time delta: applies one globally selected animation with configured speed, refreshes transforms, every skin palette and material buffer, then boosts texture demand to a fixed 2048² rather than projected face area. Sources: `indra\newview\gltfscenemanager.cpp:318-339,362-378,478-491`; `indra\newview\llvovolume.cpp:1357-1358`; `indra\newview\gltf\asset.cpp:449-491`.
- **Transforms/joints:** asset-to-agent includes object scale, render rotation and position, unlike the scale-free volume drawable transform. Node asset matrices recursively compose parent×local and cache their inverse; updates traverse **all scenes**, not just `mScene`. Nonrigged node palettes pack affine matrices into 12 floats per node, capped by `getMaxGLTFJointCount()`. Skins pack `joint.assetMatrix × inverseBindMatrix` likewise into their own palettes; shader draws choose node ID or bind the node's skin palette. The caller provides camera×asset model-view. Sources: `indra\newview\llviewerobject.cpp:4579-4589`; `indra\newview\gltf\asset.cpp:95-188`; `indra\newview\gltf\animation.cpp:402-468`; `indra\newview\gltfscenemanager.cpp:625-632,690-695,723-745`.
- **Material buffer:** 12 vec4s per material; five independent texture-transform/UV-channel pairs cover base, normal, metallic-roughness, emissive and **independent occlusion**. Remaining records contain emissive RGB, roughness, metallic, MASK cutoff or -1. Base-color factor is multiplied into 8-bit vertex colors at primitive preparation, not included in this material block. Material count is truncated to uniform-block capacity, while the consumer uses original material indices; no overflow fallback appears in this chain. Parsed normal scale and occlusion strength are not encoded by this uploader. Sources: `indra\newview\gltf\asset.cpp:191-237`; `indra\newview\gltf\asset.h:96-159`; `indra\newview\gltf\primitive.cpp:422-439`.
- **Textures:** native binding uses all five channels, unlike SL ORM aliasing. Missing images bind white/flat-normal defaults; shadow mode binds base color only. UV channel selection honors `KHR_texture_transform` override. Sampler wrap and magnification are applied; minification deliberately follows viewer preference, not the imported sampler. Sources: `indra\newview\gltfscenemanager.cpp:752-832`; `indra\newview\gltf\asset.cpp:1201-1216`.
- **Primitive preparation:** reads position/normal/tangent/color, UV0/UV1 and only JOINTS_0/WEIGHTS_0. Missing indices become sequential, missing colors become white, normals/tangents can be generated; points/lines without normals become unlit. Variant flags derive from weights, unlit material, nonzero selected UV channel and BLEND mode; MASK shares the nonblend family. The retained upload helper packs separate joints/weights, flips UV Y for GPU data only and rebases 32-bit indices. No morph-target deformation participates in this preparation/update chain. Sources: `indra\newview\gltf\primitive.cpp:326-597`; `indra\newview\gltf\animation.cpp:91-124`.
- **Consumer ordering/visibility:** manager visits registration-order objects without group frustum/occlusion/distance tests; recursively dispatches multi-UV first, then single-UV. Within an asset: single-sided then double-sided; material-index buckets then their primitive-list order. Alpha is not depth-sorted here. Hardware/shader availability can skip the entire consumer. Nonrigged binds node/material buffers; rigged binds material plus per-node skin. Sources: `indra\newview\gltfscenemanager.cpp:578-750`; `indra\newview\gltf\asset.h:330-349`. This is a latent policy, because the draw lists are unpopulated at baseline.
- **Pool hook sequence:** PBR-mask deferred invokes nonrigged lit native opaque/MASK before SL PBR batches; **both** PBR pool instances invoke rigged lit native opaque/MASK after their static SL batches. Fullbright-mask post invokes unlit native opaque/MASK, static then rigged. Post-water alpha stock/interleaved invokes native lit static→rigged then unlit static→rigged before ordinary rigged/interleaved alpha, with explicit depth writes. Despite its “to depth buffer” comment, this stock hook also inherits `forwardRender`'s RGB+alpha writes—it is not locally color-disabled. OIT paths have a separate same-order `drawGLTFScene()` hook; native consumers do not enter the volume PPLL/peel draw-info lists. Sources: `indra\newview\lldrawpoolpbropaque.cpp:53-68`; `indra\newview\lldrawpoolsimple.cpp:184-190`; `indra\newview\lldrawpoolalpha.cpp:420-462,489-500`. Apparent duplicate native rigged dispatch and unsorted/native-alpha behavior are dormant until production is restored, not reasons to reproduce them in a new renderer.
- **CPU picking remains prepared:** primitives build triangle/strip/fan octrees from CPU positions even though GPU batching is disabled; lines/points have no pick volume. Manager picking iterates registered assets regardless of object bounds, converts agent→asset→node-local, returns closest node/primitive hit, UV0/normal/tangent, and transforms the result back. The object helper accepts face and transparency/rigging/selectability flags but does not use those arguments to filter intersections. Picking uses original positions, not skin-deformed vertices; node animation is reflected by matrices, skin deformation is not. Sources: `indra\newview\gltf\primitive.cpp:535-537,635-767`; `indra\newview\gltf\asset.cpp:240-329`; `indra\newview\gltfscenemanager.cpp:843-973`.

**Native Vulkan scope decision:** restoring native-scene GPU draw-data production is a feature/restoration beyond baseline visual parity. If undertaken, use the recovered assets/nodes/materials/skin/picking contract, but explicitly fix capacity handling, alpha ordering, supported deformation/picking policy and view eligibility rather than treating these dormant paths as a mature reference renderer. Shader arithmetic quirks are covered separately by the shader report.

## 7. What not to preserve blindly / verification gaps

1. **Inherited state is real.** Most pools do not explicitly establish every depth/blend/write/stencil setting. No `glStencil*` or explicit stencil-test usage occurs in the inspected draw-pool implementations; stencil scheduling must be recovered from the parent/backend reports. Do not infer “no stencil” globally from these consumers.
2. **Obsolete names are not behavior.** `POOL_ALPHA` is not a pool; old material-specific blend enums are redirected to shared alpha; old sky is empty; avatar forward skinning is preview-only; terrain fixed-function routines are not in its modern entry chain. Conversely, **legacy material appearance, shiny, emboss bump and alpha-only water exclusion remain active compatibility content**, not dead merely because their data is old.
3. **Native GLTF scene assets are a separate, currently incomplete GPU path.** Volume rebuild/LOD skips `mGLTFAsset`; several pools call `GLTFSceneManager` directly. The targeted follow-up in §6a closes this scope: CPU preparation/update/picking exist, but native render-list population and geometry upload are compile-disabled. Do not confuse these dormant mesh draws with active SL PBR volume materials.
4. **DoF and water exclusions need captures.** The DoF depth-only intention is not proved by the local implementation; water exclusion repeats the same plane list with two obsolete pass numbers. These are observed compatibility caveats, not changes proposed here.
5. **Runtime settings remain unverified.** Default constructor/cache fallback values are cited where relevant, but actual user settings, shader support, material assets, loaded texture state, OIT availability, reflection mode and inherited render state were not sampled.
6. **Ordering fidelity should be tested with authored scenes:** crossing water surfaces; partially transparent legacy-mask materials; PBR double-sided masks; linked moving alpha faces; overlapping rigged/unrigged avatar attachments; TE glow behind alpha; custom-blend particles beside OIT; unloaded/returning avatars; and animesh animation bounds.

The native Vulkan scene interface should therefore expose stable scene identity; current geometry/LOD and update version; coordinate-space transform; material family/coverage and cutoff; optional skin palette; per-draw custom blending; water-side/view eligibility; base versus glow/bump/exclusion contribution; and group/avatar ordering keys. Preserve these observable decisions before optimizing submission or replacing the octree/query mechanism.
